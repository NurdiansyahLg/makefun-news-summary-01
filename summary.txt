Wednesday 11 November 2026 — world news in brief.

Democrats won both US chambers in the midterms — 254 House seats to 163, at least 54 in the Senate — the AP reported, with 16 races uncalled. The first US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks in nine months opened in Abu Dhabi; day one ran over four hours, ended without a deal, but both sides called it constructive and resume today. Three oil tankers caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz after striking mines; monitors counted 37 attacks on shipping there in November, the most since the war began, and Iran said the strait stays shut until Washington answers its seven conditions for talks. The US House voted 211-204 to send the impeachment case against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing it. British police arrested a US Army officer on suspicion of spying for Russia, and US prosecutors charged three men over an alleged plot against RAF Fairford. The DR Congo reported 11,042 Ebola cases and 5,387 deaths, the second-largest outbreak on record. In Yemen, government forces and air strikes hit Houthi positions in six provinces; the WHO counts 1,771 killed since August and over 300,000 displaced. Anthropic set a $70-$78 range for its Nasdaq listing, which could raise $64.4bn and value the AI lab at up to $1.4tn fully diluted, CNBC and Reuters reported. US prices rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% expected, making a December Fed rate rise look all but certain. Nvidia shareholders sued the company and chief Jensen Huang over a Chinese spying scandal, days after a defence-linked Chinese university was found to have bought two H200 chips. In India, Delhi's air improved only slightly to an average index of 299, while prices of 137 cancer drugs were cut by up to 70%. Israel's new Knesset was sworn in after an inconclusive election. World leaders opened the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkiye, pushing electrification and a move from fossil fuels; the US stayed away for a second year.
